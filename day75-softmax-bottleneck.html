🧩 The softmax bottleneck — a language model turns a hidden vector into V logits through one matrix, so the whole matrix of logits it can produce has rank at most the width of that vector, and the set of distributions it can represent is pinned by an integer. One matrix, and everything it cannot say

The last thing a language model does is turn a hidden vector into a score for every symbol in the vocabulary. That is one matrix multiply: z = W h, with h of width d and W of shape V × d. Stack the logits for every context you might ever feed it and you get a matrix whose rank cannot exceed d, no matter how deep, how nonlinear or how well trained everything underneath is. A product of matrices cannot have more rank than its thinnest factor.

That is the whole mechanism. Everything else on the MEASURE tab is an attempt to find out what it costs and whether anything you would normally look at can see it.

The capacity is the rank of the log odds, not of log P

Softmax is blind to a constant added to a row. Two logit matrices that differ by c⋅1ᵀ give the same distribution, so what a head actually controls is the matrix of log odds against some reference symbol, log p(y|c) − log p(y₀|c). The reachable set is exactly “rank at most d, with the reference column zero”, and the target on this page is built in that same gauge on purpose.

The reference symbol looks like a choice and is not one: the page recomputes the rank in all twelve gauges and gets the same integer every time. That is worth checking rather than believing, because it is what makes the phrase “the log-odds rank” well defined.

The rule that usually gets quoted is one step removed: rank(log P) ≤ d + 1. That version is necessary, and the extra one is not free capacity — it is spent on the log partition function, one rank-one column that normalisation puts there and no model can use for anything else. The two rules coincide exactly when the all-ones vector lies in the row space of log P, and the page measures that distance: on forty randomly drawn tasks it is at most 4.1e-15, which is why the loose version survives in print. Build the exception on purpose — no more contexts than rank — and the quoted rule waves through a width whose own truncation still costs 0.0485 nats.

The obstruction is continuous, and “rank” is the wrong word for it

Rank is an integer. A matrix whose seventh singular value is 1e-9 has rank seven and is, for every purpose a model has, rank six. So a criterion phrased as a rank inequality is a step function laid over something smooth, and the MEASURE tab prices that mismatch.

Over 120 trained configurations the rank gap ranks final quality at AUC 0.737, which is real but blunt. Hold the true rank and the width fixed — the control — and it scores exactly 0.500, because within one cell it is a constant. It is not noisy there; it is silent, and the outcomes it has been asked to arbitrate still span 513×. The continuous quantity the criterion discards, the tail of the singular spectrum below the cut, scores 0.906, and as a fraction of the whole spectrum 0.922. Neither needs a training step.

This is the sense in which the softmax bottleneck is real and its usual statement is not useful: the bottleneck is a fact about a matrix, and the number you want is how much of that matrix falls off the edge, not whether anything does.

The optimum in logit space is not the best model

There is a genuine closed form here. Eckart–Young says the best rank-d approximation of a matrix in the Frobenius norm is its truncated SVD, and because the reachable set is exactly the rank-d matrices in this gauge, that truncation is the exact optimum of a well-posed problem. The page does not stop at computing it: it solves one linear system to put that bottleneck inside a real tanh network, and the network's excess matches the direct computation to 1.1e-16 with a solve residual never above 2.1e-15. At any width that can represent the task the truncation is the target itself and the built network scores 6.1e-17 — the optimum written down rather than searched for.

And then training beats it, by 39.2% at the default width and 56.1% at the widest bottlenecked one. Nothing is wrong with Eckart–Young; it is optimal in the norm it is optimal in, and the Frobenius norm of the logits is not the loss. A KL cares about where the probability mass is, and it will happily accept a much larger logit error on symbols the true conditional has already decided are unlikely. So the exact answer to the neighbouring question is a bound on this one, and a loose one.

The same table also says what is not the limit at a sufficient width. The constructed network scores 6.1e-17 and the trained one scores 7.45e-5 at the same width — twelve orders of magnitude of pure optimiser. When a model at an adequate width underperforms, the ceiling is not the thing to blame.

Nothing below the bottleneck helps

If a floor is set by an integer, the usual levers should not move it, and they do not. Twelve times the parameters below the bottleneck does not lower it — the best of the five is one of the two smallest networks, at 292 parameters, and the largest at 2,056 is 1.60× worse, which is the optimiser having a harder time and not extra capacity paying off. Forty times the training budget moves it by 2.14×, and from 500 steps on the curve is flat to 1.4%.

That is what makes the bottleneck an unpleasant failure: it produces a smooth converged-looking curve at a wrong answer, and every diagnostic you would reach for — train longer, go wider underneath — comes back saying the model is fine.

The mixture works, and here you should not use it

The published fix is a mixture of softmaxes: compute K bottleneck vectors, run K softmaxes, and average them with context-dependent weights. The logarithm now sits outside a sum, so the log-probability matrix is no longer low rank at all, and the reachable log-odds rank rises to about K⋅d without d changing. It works here: a mixture at width 3 is 18.1× better than one softmax at the same width.

The control is the honest half. The cheapest single softmax that beats that mixture is width 6, with 189 fewer parameters, and it is a further 51× better. On this page more width simply wins, and the reason is arithmetic rather than opinion: the page takes the price of one more unit of reachable rank straight from its own parameter lists, and gets 37 parameters by widening against 33.3 by mixing — a wash. Widening is cheap here because the vocabulary is twelve symbols wide.

Put GPT-2 small's shape into the same two expressions and it inverts: 51,026 parameters per rank by widening against 770 by mixing, a factor of 66. The mixture is not a better model, it is a cheaper way to buy the same integer — and it only becomes worth buying that way when the output matrix is large enough to make width expensive. A method whose case rests on a price should be argued with a price.

What to take away

  • The capacity of a softmax head is the rank of the log-odds matrix, and it is d. The familiar d + 1 is about log P, is necessary rather than sufficient, and the extra dimension is spent on normalisation.
  • The criterion is an integer; the cost is continuous. Measure the tail of the spectrum, not whether it is empty.
  • The best rank-d logits are a closed form, and they are not the best model. Optimal in the wrong norm is still not optimal.
  • Capacity below the bottleneck and training budget do not move the floor, which is exactly why a bottlenecked run looks healthy.
  • A mixture of softmaxes buys reachable rank without buying width. Whether that is a good trade is a parameter count, and at a twelve-symbol vocabulary it is not.

Building it, in the order the page does

1 · Build the target in the gauge the model lives in

The reachable set is exactly “log-odds matrices of rank at most d whose reference column is zero”. So the target is built there directly: an orthonormal basis in context space, another in symbol space whose first coordinate is zeroed, and a spectrum picked by hand. That makes the singular values of the target a choice rather than a measurement, and makes Eckart–Young apply on the nose.

var Ub = orthoBasis(N, R, rnd, false);      // R directions over contexts
var Wb = orthoBasis(V, R, rnd, true);      // R directions over symbols, first entry 0
for (k = 0; k < R; k++) s[k] = scale * Math.pow(decay, k);
D[b][j] += s[k] * Ub[k][b] * Wb[k][j];     // first column exactly 0, spectrum exactly s

Then p*(y|c) = softmax(D row), and because the whole context space is enumerated, the conditional entropy, the mutual information and every model's excess are exact expectations. The teacher's own excess is 0 with no tolerance attached.

2 · Get the rank right, twice

Rank questions are decided by small singular values, so the SVD is one-sided Jacobi — plane rotations until every pair of columns is orthogonal — which computes small singular values to high relative accuracy. The threshold is relative to the largest singular value, never absolute.

rank_softmax(A) = rank(A - A[:,j] 1ᵀ)     // the same integer for EVERY j
rank_softmax(A) = rank(A) - (1ᵛ in rowspace(A) ? 1 : 0)

Those two are computed by different routes — a re-gauged matrix through the SVD, and a projection of the all-ones vector onto the row space — and the page asserts they agree, in all twelve gauges. If they ever disagreed, the phrase “the log-odds rank” would not mean anything.

3 · One model with a knob, not two models

The single softmax and the mixture are the same forward pass with K = 1 and K > 1. That is what makes the comparison a comparison, and it comes with a free identity: a mixture of one softmax must be that softmax, bit for bit.

for (i = 0; i < V; i++){
  var sp = 0;
  for (k = 0; k < K; k++) sp += pi[k] * sm[k][i];
  p[i] = sp;                               // K = 1 -> pi[0] === 1 -> p === sm[0]
}

4 · A backward pass a mixture actually needs

For a single softmax under cross entropy the gradient at the logits is the familiar p − target. A mixture breaks that shortcut: the incoming signal has to be split across components by their weights first, and each component then carries its own softmax Jacobian.

dp[i]  = -pstar[i] / p[i] / N;                        // through the mixture
dsk[i] = dp[i] * pi[k];                               // into component k
dz[i]  = sm[k][i] * (dsk[i] - dot);                   // its own softmax Jacobian
dg[k]  = pi[k] * (dpi[k] - pdot);                     // and into the gate

5 · Check it with something exact, at initialisation

A second forward pass is written over dual numbers — value and derivative together, one seed per parameter, no step size anywhere — which traverses the graph in the opposite direction to the backward pass. It agrees to 9.57e-16 relative to the largest gradient, while a central difference manages 3.78e-10, and it reproduces the loss bit for bit. All of it at initialisation: checking at convergence is how you get a false failure, because the true gradient there is already smaller than any difference quotient can resolve.

6 · Write the optimum down instead of searching for it

Truncate the target's SVD at d, scale the resulting bottleneck into (-1, 1) so a tanh can produce it, take the inverse hyperbolic tangent, and solve one least-squares system for the layer that has to hit it.

Ht[b][k] = alpha * Ub[k][b];                 // the bottleneck we want
W3[j][k] = s[k] * Wb[k][j] / alpha;          // the head that reads it
tgt[b][k] = atanh(Ht[b][k]);
X = lstsq([a | 1], tgt);                     // one solve, residual ~1e-15

The result is a real network, not a formula: its excess matches the direct computation to 9.7e-17 at the default width. At a width that suffices it is 6.1e-17, twelve orders of magnitude below what training reaches, which is how the page can say that at those widths the ceiling is not what is stopping anyone.

7 · Controls that run where the claim lives

Both “what is not the limit” controls clamp their width to a genuinely bottlenecked one. A control that runs at a width which already suffices measures the optimiser and reports it as a property of the bottleneck — the excess there is 1e-5 and its ratios are noise with a decimal point in them.

8 · Assert identities, not thresholds

The page's self-check is 492 assertions and the load-bearing ones are equalities: the target's reference column is === 0; every gauge returns the same integer; the rank of log P is the log-odds rank plus a row-space indicator, both directions; the truncation's tail and head add up to the whole; the constructed network is the truncation to 1e-12; a mixture of one softmax is its component with a gap of === 0; the price of a width is === V + hid + 1 taken from the parameter lists; and the rank gap's within-cell AUC is === 0.5, because a constant predictor ties every pair. A threshold chosen to pass at one slider setting is a failure waiting for a different one.
